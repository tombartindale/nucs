NUCS / Beacon UI · design exploration

Three ways to show what's done and what to do next

Three design agents worked from one shared brief. Each wrote a concept and a clickable static mockup. None of the app code was changed.

User: producer who does the work Order: module delivery dates (planning.ts) Mandarin: equal to English Constraint: no pipeline logic in the UI

The three directions

A · a-action-queue

Next-Action Queue

The landing page is one to-do list. Each row has a verb, a scope, why it's there, a due date and a button, grouped across topics and sorted by deadline slack. Sections are Today, This week, Later, Waiting and Unscheduled.

Strong at

  • Answers "what do I do right now" literally
  • Jobs run inside their row, and the topics move to their next row when it finishes
  • The "why?" toggle shows the arithmetic behind a row's position
  • English and Mandarin actions in one list

Weak at

  • The overall picture is squeezed into one strip
  • The order rests on rough effort guesses
  • Doesn't show whether the whole plan is achievable
Open mockup
B · b-delivery-runway

Delivery Runway

The landing page is a timeline with a "today" line and one lane per module (English and Mandarin sub-lanes) running to delivery. Lateness, spare time and clashes show up as shapes. "What now" is whatever the today line crosses.

Strong at

  • Shows that no time is scheduled for editing or translation after recording
  • A "your days" strip shows two modules needing the same day
  • Drills down to a session sheet per unit
  • Includes a concrete fix for undated Mandarin work, with a preview toggle

Weak at

  • Makes rough estimates look precise
  • Below unit level it's really a checklist
  • Problems with no date attached feel less important
Open mockup
C · c-flow-board

Pipeline Flow Board

Stage columns with English and Mandarin swimlanes. Cards are modules, or units once you filter. The boundary between two columns carries its action ("38 ready · Render 38"), and a red "needs you" shelf collects blocked work.

Strong at

  • Shows where work is piling up (e.g. 96 topics waiting to be recorded)
  • One click moves a whole batch of ready topics on
  • Folds Translation into the board itself
  • Reading rule: right to left, red first

Weak at

  • Gives an exact next step less precisely
  • Needs about 1,300px of width
  • One urgent topic can hide inside a module card

Data gaps all three agents found

  • Mandarin has no dates. planning.ts gives the translated and packaged milestones no due date. All three propose a translator turnaround setting, and B adds an edit/post-production buffer on module_plans.
  • No time after recording. The last recording session lands on delivery minus 1, leaving zero days for cues, packaging and translation. A module can read "on track" when it isn't.
  • No record of how long work has been waiting. bcn status doesn't report when a topic reached its stage or when a translation batch was exported (stage_since, exported_at).
  • Modules are planned in isolation. Nothing adds up your hours across modules, so two "on track" modules can need the same day. /api/plans would also need to return per-unit tasks.

Questions for you

  1. Which landing page fits your Monday morning: a list of next actions (A), a timeline (B) or a stage board (C)? Or one as the landing page with another as a tab? For example, A's queue on the landing page with C's board or B's runway on the module page.
  2. Should I fix the Mandarin and post-recording scheduling gap first, since all three depend on it? It means turnaround and edit-buffer settings in module_plans, with planning.ts extended to schedule them.
  3. Is adding up your hours across modules (B's "your days" strip) important? It's the one thing only B shows.
  4. Next step: a merged concept, or a working prototype of one direction as a new route in the Vue app beside the existing pages?
